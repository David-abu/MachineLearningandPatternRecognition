\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Convergence to the optimum.} Test P(error) falls steadily with training-set size: 0.2508 at $N=100$, 0.1807 at $N=500$, 0.1523 at $N=1000$, 0.1501 at $N=5000$ and 0.1496 at $N=10000$, against 0.1484 for the true-pdf MAP classifier. The 95\% Wilson intervals (half-width $\approx0.0022$; see the figure) of the $N=5000$ and $N=10000$ models overlap the optimal classifier's interval. The trained MLPs therefore approach the Bayes decision rule, as expected of ML posterior estimates from a sufficiently flexible model.
\item \textbf{What cross-validation selects.} Very small networks underfit badly for every $N$: $P=1$ gives a CV error of about 0.45, because one hidden unit maps $\mathbb R^3$ onto a single scalar and cannot carve out four regions, and $P=2$ gives about 0.27. From $P=3$ upward the CV curve for large $N$ is essentially flat at the Bayes level. Three smooth ramps are already enough to approximate the four quadratic-boundary regions well, so the extra capacity neither helps nor hurts once data are plentiful. For small $N$ the curve is U-shaped: with 100 samples it rises from 0.18 at $P=3$ to 0.26--0.29 at larger $P$. Unregularized ML training of a large network on few points fits noise. The $N=100$ final model reaches a training error of 0.07 but a test error of 0.25.
\item \textbf{Selected sizes need not grow monotonically with $N$.} The selected $P$ is 3, 6, 3, 3, 12. For $N\ge1000$ the CV errors of all $P\ge3$ lie within about 0.005 of each other, comparable to the CV estimate's own sampling noise ($\sqrt{0.15\cdot0.85/N}\approx0.004$--$0.011$). Which $P$ wins is therefore partly chance; what matters is that every candidate $P\ge3$ performs at nearly the Bayes level. The $N=10000$ choice of $P=12$ (CV error 0.150, versus 0.153 for $P=3$) is consistent with more data supporting a slightly richer model, and its test error is the lowest of the five.
\item \textbf{Sources of the remaining gap.} At $N=100$ and $500$ the gap to optimal (0.10 and 0.03) is estimation error: too few samples to pin down 28--52 parameters by unpenalized ML. At $N\ge5000$ the remaining gap of at most 0.0017 is within test-set sampling uncertainty. The training sets are independent draws rather than nested subsets, so the learning curve is not guaranteed to be monotone, though here it is.
\end{itemize}
